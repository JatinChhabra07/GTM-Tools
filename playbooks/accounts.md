# Accounts playbook

Working one company (an account) with possibly many people (contacts). Plan §4.1 is the source of
truth; these rules are enforced in code by the gates and the global ledger. Adapted, with credit,
from Harvey's `skills/account_navigation.md` (MIT, see `NOTICE`).

## One contact per account

One active contact per account at a time, across every product. Emailing two people at the same
company at once looks like spam and they compare notes.

## Waiting between contacts

The next contact at an account goes out only 5 business days after the previous sequence
finished with no reply. At most 3 silent contacts per account per 90 days.

## When an account closes

A reply, an opt-out or a bounce from anyone at the account closes it for 90 days, in every
product. The exception: a person named in a reply ("talk to Priya") becomes a referral lead,
for that person only.

## Who to contact first

Pick the person most likely to feel the problem, able to evaluate a fix, and likely to read
email. As a rough guide by deal size:

| Deal size | First contact | Next | Avoid first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (under $500/mo) | the manager who'd use it | their boss | the CEO |
| Mid-market | director or VP | the manager who'd use it | individual contributors |
| Enterprise | VP or C-level | a manager who will champion it | junior staff |

Titles in the ICP are literal business-card titles.

## Generic inboxes

`info@`, `hello@` and similar are used only when printed on the company's own site, no named
person was found, and the address verifies. Those emails greet the team, not a name.

## Data quality

Never fabricate a contact detail. A guessed email pattern is only usable after verification.
Every fact about a person or company has a source URL.
